Levia · cost-based pricing and funding plan

What Levia costs us, and what customers pay

Prices are built from the full cost of producing Levia and running the service, plus a margin. Two offers: a single service, where Levia pays the water, detergent and electricity, and a continuous plan for offices that keep Levia, where the customer supplies those products, so the price is lower. All figures are estimates for discussion: open “Assumptions” to change any of them and every table updates.

Estimates, not quotes. Hardware costs assume production volumes from 2036; they must be validated with suppliers and with the pilot programme in France.

5 · For investors

Funding the first production

Levia is capital-intensive at the start: we build the robots and keep them on our balance sheet, then earn back the cost through the service. Equity funds development and launch; the installed fleet is financed with leasing or asset-backed debt.

How much investors put in: about €20.5M of equity

€9.5M to build, certify and launch in France (pre-seed, seed, Series A), plus about €11M in a Series B to reach break-even. The robots placed at customers (about 3,500 by 2040, roughly €24.5M of hardware at €7,000 each) are not paid with equity: they are financed with leasing or asset-backed loans and repaid by the monthly fees, since each installation pays back its hardware in about 22 months.
